Research

Nanodiscs as drug delivery vehicles

Nanodiscs are small patches of lipid bilayer, about 10–13 nm across, held in solution by a belt of membrane scaffold protein (MSP). My dissertation work in Dr. Wade Zeno's lab at USC uses MSP1 and MSP1E3D1 nanodiscs built on DMPC bilayers. I study how small-molecule chemotherapeutics can be carried in that bilayer, and I test the resulting formulations for cytotoxicity in HeLa and SUM159 cancer cells.

Loading drugs into a carrier with no aqueous core

Liposomal drugs like Doxil use ammonium sulfate or pH gradients to load doxorubicin into an internal water compartment. Nanodiscs have no such compartment, so I look at what actually controls how a drug partitions into a flat bilayer.

Choosing the drug to fit the carrier

I screen anthracyclines by hydrophobicity, optical properties and activity against solid-tumor cell lines to find cargo that suits a bilayer-only carrier.

Research built for student teams

I design projects that undergraduates and master's students can run and own, from nanodisc assembly and purification to cell assays.
